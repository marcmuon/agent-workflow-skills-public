# project-card examples

Illustrative: Shelf, a private reading tracker on the owner's phone. The project and every quote here are made up to show the shape. Real cards distinguish owner quotes, selected options and accepted summaries. Use the examples for structure, not as answers to a real interview.

## A goal row

Weak, in the interviewer's words, with nothing to judge against:

| 1 | Easy to log books | fits the owner | Logging is fast |

Strong, in their words, with their reference and a live status:

| 1 | "i want to log a book in like two taps" (Q1) | Unacceptable: "typing the title in by hand" / Great: "scan the barcode and its done" (Q1) | fits the owner | On their phone: scan → saved in two taps for their 5 first-look books (Q3), against Letterboxd's log button, their reference (Q5) | moving (#4) |

A side they did not answer stays `idk`, with affected work in Owner decides. If they only picked an offered label, record `selected option: Old history is missing (Q2)`. The goal can use their earlier quote, "keep my 120 books and their ratings"; the selection is not a new original quote. Any proposed interpretation remains a proposal until accepted.

## Questions

Goals move 1 through a question tool usable in the current mode, or one plain-chat question if none is usable. Free text and selected labels are recorded separately with their question IDs. Every call, including draft review, uses the same 12-call budget.

- "When you first open Shelf, what would make you say 'unacceptable'? What would make it great?" Optional prompts: "Logging takes too long" · "My old Goodreads history is missing" · "It looks like a spreadsheet". If they pick two, ask which wins a trade-off; both remain requirements unless they drop one.

The output, shown before it's asked about: two options whose previews are 12-line ASCII screens of the home screen (one list-first, one cover-grid-first), asking "Which is closer, and what's wrong with it?"

Pressure pass on the rank-1 goal: "You said two taps. Which app already logs things the way Shelf should?" Options stay open ("I'll name one" · "None comes to mind"), so the app they name is theirs.

If call 12 arrives with that pass or the decision boundary unanswered, save the draft and open items. Do not spend unlimited extra calls reviewing the draft. The first-look slice may be 5 books; the done output still includes all 120 requested books and their ratings.

## Decision boundary

- Run may decide: the barcode library, the storage format, layout within the direction they picked.
- Ask the owner first: importing or deleting their Goodreads history, anything that costs money.

## Progress and later observation

Record this result in CARD.md's Run log, which holds the milestone's actual output:

`2026-10-19: #4 checked, remains open. G1 moved: scan → saved in 2 taps on 5 books. G2 unchanged. G3 passing: year view shows 2025. G4 slipped: import drops ratings; repair required before #4 closes.`

If the only remaining goal is "I can use it for a week without fiddling", record `awaiting evidence (the owner, 2026-10-27)` and pause with that report as the resume condition. The delivered tracker is recorded, and the future goal stays unpassed.

For an import already performed, the read-only reviewer checks its receipt and reads back the saved books and ratings. Running the import again would create a new effect; use an authorized fixture for a repeatable test.
